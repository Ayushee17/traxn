Brand and product positioning

Make your product easier to understand and easier to buy.

Positioning is the decision about who you serve, what you replace and why you win. Get it right and every page, pitch and campaign gets easier. Get it wrong and no amount of marketing spend fixes it.

Customer understanding→Differentiation→Value proposition→Messaging

1 · The problem

Good products with blurry stories lose to worse products with clear ones.

It shows up in small ways first. A prospect reads the homepage and asks what you actually do. Sales describes the product one way, the founder another, the website a third. You're selling to three segments because narrowing feels risky, and so you're nobody's obvious choice.

Then it shows up in the numbers: long sales cycles, discounting to close, and a win rate that doesn't improve however much you spend on demand.

The root cause is usually the same. Nobody has decided, with evidence, who the best-fit customer is and what the product means to them.

2 · What it achieves

One story, agreed and written down.

A chosen customer

The segment you win most often, described specifically enough that sales can qualify against it.

A clear alternative

What the buyer compares you with: a spreadsheet, an agency, a competitor, or doing nothing.

Proven difference

Two or three things only you do, and the outcome they create in the buyer's words.

Shared language

A one-page messaging guide the whole company uses, from homepage to sales deck to job posts.

3 · Our process

Two to three weeks, four stages.

Understand · Week 1

Listen to buyers

We review your customer calls, lost-deal notes, G2 and Capterra reviews, support tickets and competitor sites. Where the evidence is thin, we interview five to eight customers and lost prospects ourselves.

Define · Week 1–2

Rank the options

We bring two or three positioning options to a 75-minute workshop, each with its best-fit customer, alternative and risk. Your team chooses a direction.

Build · Week 2

Write the guide

A one-page messaging guide, then rewritten homepage copy that proves the position in the first screen. Reviewed live with you, revised once.

Learn · Week 3+

Roll out and test

A 30-day rollout list for sales decks, outbound and ads, plus the two signals that tell you the new position is working.

5 · Who it suits

Best when the product has outgrown the story.

A good fit: B2B SaaS and AI companies from seed to Series B with paying customers and at least 20 customer conversations to learn from. Founder-led teams where the founder still shapes the pitch. Companies preparing for a US, UK or GCC launch who need the story to travel.

Probably too early: pre-revenue products still finding their first customers. Positioning needs evidence, and the fastest way to get it is to sell. We'll say so on the first call.

7 · Questions

Asked before most positioning projects.

How is this different from a brand agency?

A brand agency decides how you look and sound. Positioning decides who you're for and why you win, which has to come first. We hand our output to designers; we don't replace them.

Do you interview our customers?

If you have recorded calls and lost-deal notes, we start there and often don't need to. If the evidence is thin, we interview five to eight customers and lost prospects. Interviews add about a week.

How much of our time does it take?

Six to eight hours over two to three weeks: two workshops, one live review and a few async comments. Bring the two to four people who know your customers best.

What if the team can't agree on a direction?

That's what the evidence is for. Options come with the buyer quotes and lost-deal patterns that support them. In our experience, disagreement is usually about missing evidence, not taste.

What does it cost?

Positioning engagements are fixed-price, with a scope and price agreed before work starts. The price depends on the number of segments or products in scope and whether we run interviews. You get a one-page proposal after the first call.
