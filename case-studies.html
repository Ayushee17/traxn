Illustrative · not client workPositioning · Market entry

A composite scenario: an Indian contract-management SaaS, ₹20 crore revenue, preparing a UK launch.

The same product, positioned for Bengaluru and for London

At home the product wins against spreadsheets and email: the pitch is "stop losing contracts in inboxes". In the UK the buyer already has a tool, or a legal-ops lead who has evaluated three. The alternative changes, so everything downstream changes.

Bengaluru

Alternative
Email, shared drives, spreadsheets
Proof expected
Founder demo, a few local logos
Price anchor
Cost of one missed renewal
Headline that works
"Never lose a contract in an inbox again"

London

Alternative
An incumbent CLM tool, or a legal-ops evaluation already underway
Proof expected
UK or EU logos, security and data-residency answers, a G2 presence
Price anchor
The incumbent's per-seat fee
Headline that works
"CLM for the mid-market, at a third of the enterprise price, live in two weeks"
Insight
The home-market headline describes a problem UK buyers solved years ago. The differentiator that travels is speed and price against a known incumbent, not the problem itself.
Learning
Market entry is a positioning problem before it is a channel problem. Find the alternative in the new market first; the message follows.

Illustrative · not client workDemand generation · Measurement

A composite scenario: a 12-person marketing team running content, outbound and two events a quarter.

A measurement model for a small SaaS marketing team

The team reported traffic, followers and MQLs. Sales reported pipeline. Nobody could connect the two, so every budget review became an argument. The fix was a spreadsheet, not a data warehouse.

Insight
Of the deals created in a quarter, the first touch was recoverable for roughly three-quarters by asking one question on the first call and tagging the CRM. That was enough to see which programmes created pipeline and which created activity.
Strategy
Three programme-level metrics (qualified first calls, pipeline created, cost per qualified call), reviewed monthly, with one decision per review: scale, fix or stop.
Learning
Attribution doesn't need to be perfect to be useful. It needs to be consistent and honest about its gaps.

How we do this in a demand engagement

Original research

What AI assistants say about B2B SaaS categories.

An ongoing study. We run a fixed set of buyer prompts across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini and record which vendors are named, how they're described and which sources the answers draw on.

Method

Twenty prompts per category, written the way buyers ask (role, company size, market). Each run three times on different days. We log vendors named, position, description and cited or paraphrased sources.

Limits

Answers vary with wording, user context, location and time. Our results are a repeatable sample, not a ranking. We publish the prompts so anyone can re-run them.

Status

First categories in progress: contract management, HR tech and customer support software, for Indian and UK buyers. Findings will be published on the Insights page with the full prompt set.
