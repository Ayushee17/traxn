Go-to-market strategy

Pick the market worth winning. Then plan to win it.

A go-to-market strategy is a set of choices: which market, which buyers first, which channels, what the sales team says. We help you make those choices with evidence, and write them down so the whole company runs on the same plan.

Market selection→Audience priorities→Launch planning→Sales alignment

1 · The problem

Launches that are plans for marketing, not plans for the market.

A new product or a new region gets a launch date, a press release and a campaign. What it often doesn't get is a decision about which buyers to go after first, what they currently use instead, and what proof they'll need to switch.

The symptoms are familiar: sales and marketing working from different target lists, a pitch built for one market that doesn't land in the next, and a launch that generates attention but not pipeline.

Market entry makes this sharper. An Indian SaaS company selling into the US or UK meets different alternatives, different proof expectations and different pricing anchors. The home-market playbook rarely survives the flight.

2 · What it achieves

One plan that sales and marketing both own.

A chosen market and segment

Scored on size, access, fit and competition, with the reasons written down so the choice holds under pressure.

A localised story

Positioning, messaging and pricing hypotheses adapted to the target market's alternatives and proof expectations.

A 90-day launch plan

Two or three channels that fit your buyers, with owners, dates and the first things to ship.

An aligned sales team

Narrative, qualification criteria and objection handling that match what marketing says.

3 · Our process

Four to six weeks for a full plan. Two for a sprint.

Understand · Weeks 1–2

Research the market

Buyer interviews or desk research in the target market, competitor messaging and pricing, how AI assistants and search describe the category there, and what your existing customers in that market say.

Define · Week 3

Choose and prioritise

Market and segment scoring, then a working session to pick the first segment and the alternative you're displacing. We'll argue for focus.

Build · Weeks 4–5

Write the plan

Localised messaging, pricing hypotheses, the 90-day channel plan and an outbound playbook. Sales narrative and qualification criteria built alongside.

Learn · Week 6+

Launch and review

A launch-week checklist and a 30-, 60- and 90-day review that asks one question each time: what did we learn, and what changes?

Market entry routes

The routes we know best.

Each route has its own alternatives, proof expectations and buying habits. These are the ones we've studied most closely.

India → US

Buyers expect US logos, US-style proof and transparent pricing. Mid-market buying committees are larger than at home.

India → UK

Smaller market, more relationship-led. Compliance and data residency come up early. Understatement beats hype.

India → UAE/GCC

Partner and reseller channels matter. Government and enterprise procurement shape timing. Localisation is more than language.

US/UK → India

Price sensitivity and a preference for bundled service. Founder-led selling and regional communities carry more weight than paid media.

5 · Who it suits

For launches and market moves with something at stake.

A good fit: B2B SaaS and AI companies launching a new product line, entering the US, UK or GCC from India (or the reverse), or rebuilding how sales and marketing work together after a leadership change.

Less useful: if your positioning is still unsettled. Go-to-market builds on it. We'll usually suggest a short positioning sprint first, and we'll say so before you commit.

7 · Questions

Asked before most go-to-market projects.

Do you help with execution after the plan?

Yes, through the demand generation service, which runs the plan as a monthly programme. Many clients take the plan and run it themselves; it's written so they can.

Can you do in-market research if we have no customers there yet?

Yes. We combine desk research, competitor analysis and five to eight interviews with target buyers, which we recruit through networks and paid panels. Interviews add one to two weeks.

How do you decide which market to enter?

A scorecard with four factors: market size for your segment, how easily you can reach buyers, how well your product fits local alternatives, and how crowded the category is. We show the scores and the sources, and we'll disagree with you if the evidence does.

What does it cost?

Fixed price, agreed before work starts. It depends on the number of markets, whether we run interviews, and how many stakeholders need aligning. You get a one-page proposal after the first call.
